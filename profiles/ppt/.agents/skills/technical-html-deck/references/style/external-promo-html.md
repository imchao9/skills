# External Promotional HTML Presentation

Use this mode for public-facing product stories, launch pages, capability demos, customer-facing narratives, portfolio pages, conference microsites, and HTML presentations intended to persuade outside audiences.

## Goal

Make the value obvious in the first viewport and make the proof credible by the third section.

The artifact should answer:

- What is being offered?
- Who is it for?
- What concrete outcome does it create?
- What proof or demo makes the claim believable?
- What should the viewer do next?

## Narrative Shape

Recommended 6-9 section arc:

1. First viewport: literal offer/category, not a vague slogan.
2. Pain or before-state: one concrete audience problem.
3. Product/capability reveal: show the real product, workflow, output, or artifact.
4. Proof: screenshots, metrics, generated examples, comparison, or customer workflow.
5. How it works: three-step process or architecture-level explanation.
6. Use cases: 3-5 concrete scenarios.
7. Trust and constraints: security, limitations, deployment, compatibility.
8. Call to action: clear next step.

## CM Style Rules

- The first viewport must reveal the product, output, place, or capability directly.
- Use real screenshots or generated bitmap/product visuals when inspection matters.
- Avoid vague gradient-only hero sections.
- Do not bury the core object in tiny nav text.
- Keep copy concrete: outcomes, constraints, examples, proof.
- Use motion sparingly for section transitions or artifact reveals, not decorative noise.

## Visual Difference From Internal Decks

External promo pages can be more immersive than internal technical decks, but still must remain concrete.

- More imagery and product screenshots.
- More spatial storytelling.
- Stronger first-screen object signal.
- Shorter visible text.
- Clear CTA.
- Less internal implementation detail unless trust requires it.

## Report-Inspired Variant

When the user points to a corporate report such as the Google AI responsibility update, use it as a restraint and credibility reference:

- white canvas with generous margins.
- large literal headline with one colored keyword phrase.
- one dominant rounded proof visual instead of many small cards.
- short evidence columns and quote or metric blocks.
- quiet footer or section marker.

Do not copy brand colors, logos, assets, or high report-style text density.

## Passing Bar

The viewer should be able to say within 10 seconds:

- I know what this is.
- I know whether it is for me.
- I have seen the actual output or product signal.
- I know the next action.
